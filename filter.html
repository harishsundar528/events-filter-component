Bee Pavilion

Wednesday, 10.30 - 11.20am
Front of the entrance to Building 63 at the Lemon-Scented Lawn, 25 Exhibition Walk, Clayton Campus

Led by Associate Professor Alan Dorin, the NativeBee+ Tech Facility harnesses AI, data science, computer vision and simulation to develop new technology that monitors, simulates and improves our understanding and management of insect-plant interactions. Insect pollinators directly contribute to 34% of global food production – approximately AUD$238 billion per year. Even the tiniest drops of pollinator abundance or efficiency can cause production losses that have the potential to undermine global food security. Similarly, insect pests cause billions of dollars of crop loss annually. As the climate changes and humans spread invasive species around the globe, these issues concerning the interactions betweeen insects and plants are becoming increasingly important tackle in agriculture, and in biodiversity conservation. Integrating computer science, ecology, entomology, engineering and agriculture, this climate-controlled and digitally-wired facility is home to Australian native insect species. Here the Facility staff and students research, design and test monitoring, simulation and management tools for the good of the planet’s natural and agricultural ecosystems.

Bootcamp - Online profiles with impact - Grab an ORCiD and watch your online presence bloom!

Friday, 12 – 12.30pm
Online

A well curated online researcher profile maximises your visibility and promotes the impact of your research. An ORCID ID (Open Researcher Contributor IDentifier) is a persistent digital identifier you can set up to curate your biography, grant, employment, qualifications and publication information, in a single profile. ORCID IDs are used by researchers around the world, as well as by publishers and funders. Bootcamp - Top tips for APC free journal publishing

Friday, 1 – 1.30pm
Online

Article processing charges (APCs) are levies imposed by publishers for open access publications. Costs can differ, with some high-impact journals demanding over $10,000 AUD per article. Authors aim to disseminate their research widely, yet APCs pose a substantial financial strain on them, their institutions, and funding sources. In this session, discover strategies to sidestep APCs by leveraging transformative agreements, identifying Diamond OA journals, and other cost-saving tips.

Monash Business Behavioural Lab

Friday, 10 - 11am
Monash University Caulfield Campus,
Building S, Ground Floor
900 Dandenong Road Caulfield East, VIC 3145

Find out about neurophysiological techniques to study judgement and decision-making in a complex world. You will be able to some of the technologies commonly used in our lab, some of which are also used by researchers in real world settings. Devices measure physiological signals and link them to psychology, and include eye tracking, pupillometry, skin electrical conductivity, electrocardiography, and electroencephalography. Measurements from these devices provide unique avenues to build and test theories for various cognitive and emotional processes, such as attention, memory, stress, emotional intelligence, etc. They can be beneficial for disciplines like accounting, finance, marketing, and behavioural economics, and many more. Examples of their feasibility in complex decision-making will be presented through the effects of uncertainty visualisation on professional judgement, the role of emotional engagement in financial trading, and neurophysiological mechanisms underlying professional scepticism and compulsive buying behaviour.
